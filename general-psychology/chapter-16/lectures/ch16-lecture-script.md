### Alarm Reaction

**What it means:** The first stage of the general adaptation syndrome (GAS), characterized by sympathetic arousal and mental clarity.

**A quick way to remember it:** Think: First GAS stage: sympathetic arousal and mental clarity.

**A quick way to remember it:** Think: Resources depleted.

**For example:** After prolonged stress, physical and psychological resources become depleted.

**Why it matters:** Which GAS stage occurs when physical and psychological resources are depleted?

### General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS)

**What it means:** Hans Selye’s three-stage model for an organism’s response to stressors.

**A quick way to remember it:** Think: Alarm → resistance → exhaustion.

**For example:** Selye's model organizes the stress response into alarm, resistance, and exhaustion.

**Why it matters:** Which model describes the alarm, resistance, and exhaustion stages?

### Resistance

**What it means:** The second stage of the general adaptation syndrome (GAS), characterized by coping with ongoing stress.

**A quick way to remember it:** Think: Coping with ongoing stress.

**For example:** After the initial alarm reaction, the body continues coping with an ongoing stressor.

**Why it matters:** Which GAS stage is characterized by coping with ongoing stress?

### GAS Sequence

**What it means:** Selye's general adaptation syndrome progresses from alarm to resistance to exhaustion as a stressor persists.

**A quick way to remember it:** Think: alarm → resistance → exhaustion

**For example:** Alarm mobilizes resources; resistance reflects ongoing coping; exhaustion occurs after resources are depleted.

**Why it matters:** Put the three GAS stages in order from first to last.

### Mental Clarity During the Alarm Reaction

**What it means:** The alarm reaction brings sympathetic arousal plus mental clarity.

**A quick way to remember it:** alarm brings sharp focus

**For example:** The quiz describes the alarm reaction as characterized by sympathetic arousal and mental clarity.; The lesson says all bodily resources are mobilized during the alarm stage.

**Don't confuse it with:** Alarm: mobilization and clarity. Exhaustion: depletion and breakdown.

### Selye Expanded Cannon's Fight-or-Flight Work

**What it means:** Selye's general adaptation syndrome expanded on Cannon's earlier work on fight-or-flight.

**A quick way to remember it:** Selye built on Cannon

**For example:** The quiz feedback states that Selye expanded on Cannon's work on stress.; The chapter notes Selye's alarm reaction corresponds with Cannon's fight-or-flight state.

**Why it matters:** A test asks whose work Selye's general adaptation syndrome built upon. What is the answer?

**Don't confuse it with:** Cannon described fight-or-flight; Selye added the three-stage pattern of alarm, resistance, and exhaustion.

### Resistance vs. Resilience

**What it means:** Resistance is the body's stage of coping with ongoing stress; resilience is a person's ability to adapt to life's challenges in positive ways.

**A quick way to remember it:** resistance is not resilience

**For example:** The lesson's GAS resistance phase: the body keeps responding to stress at lower intensity while resources compensate.; The lesson defines resilience as adapting to life's challenges in positive ways — a trait linked to well-being.

**Why it matters:** A student bounces back from failure with a better plan. Is that resistance or resilience?

**Don't confuse it with:** Resistance is a stress-response stage in the body; resilience is a psychological strength of the person.
